\# AML Red Flags Case Study



\## Scenario



A customer runs a small local cash business.



The customer makes cash deposits of about $9,800 every day for one week.



The deposits are always below $10,000.



The customer says that the deposits are from the business, but does not have formal records or invoices to support the cash activity.



\## Red Flags



\- Repeated cash deposits.

\- Deposits are just below $10,000.

\- High cash activity.

\- No formal records or invoices.



\## Possible AML Concern



The activity may indicate structuring.



Structuring means breaking a large amount of money into smaller transactions to avoid reporting or monitoring requirements.



\## AML Actions



An AML Analyst may:



1\. Review the customer's transaction history.

2\. Ask questions about the source of the cash.

3\. Request supporting documents.

4\. Assess the customer's risk.

5\. Escalate the case if the activity remains suspicious.



\## Conclusion



The activity should be reviewed carefully because the repeated deposits just below $10,000 are a possible red flag for structuring.
